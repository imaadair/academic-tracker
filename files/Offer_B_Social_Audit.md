## Tampa Bay is disqualified, and you spotted it first

Their two Shorts pull **17,000 and 37,000 views** against 374 on their long-form. That's not a team dabbling — that's a produced brand campaign ("ready to live boldly") with real distribution behind it.

Combined with the studio set, lit backdrop, and designed lower-thirds you flagged as "basically a traditional ad": **someone is being paid properly to do this.** Almost certainly an agency. They're not a prospect, they're a client of your competitor.

Your instinct on the footage got there before my audit did.

## CenterState needs a caveat

Their two Shorts are titled *"Why Authentic Leadership Creates Stronger Communities"* and *"How To Build Trust and Lead With Purpose."*

Those aren't campaign spots. **Those are pull-quotes cut from Talk CNY** — the exact service you'd be selling.

But look at the ratio: **2 Shorts against 659 videos.** They've done it twice and stopped, and the results were 89 and 38 views. So the honest read is *"they tried this and it didn't stick"* rather than *"they have it covered."*

That's a harder conversation than a clean gap, but not a closed door. It does change the pitch — you'd be asking why it stopped, not announcing an opportunity.

Still your best local lead. Just go in knowing they've been here before.

## The pattern worth naming

**Every single organisation has Shorts. Not one had zero.**

Between one and two each, mostly campaign or promo content, mostly modest views. So the premise "these orgs have no short-form capability" is **false everywhere**. That's the third structural assumption in this project that hasn't survived contact.

What *is* true is narrower and still real:

> They produce one or two Shorts a year from campaigns. They have between 23 and 659 interview videos that have never been touched.

Chester County has **93 videos and two Shorts about Small Business Week.** Their founder interviews — the actual substance — have never been cut. That gap is genuine.

**So the pitch changes.** Not *"you're not doing short-form"* — they'll correct you and you'll lose the room. Instead:

> *"You've made a couple of Shorts for campaigns. You've got ninety-three interviews that have never been touched. That's where the volume is."*

It's more accurate, harder to dismiss, and it uses their existing Shorts as proof they already believe in the format.

## What I should have done from the start

Two checks, both cheap, both skipped:

1. **Shorts tab** — thirty seconds, tells you whether anyone is repurposing
2. **Shorts view counts vs. long-form** — if Shorts massively outperform, someone competent is behind them

I gave you eleven names having verified footage on two and socials on none. That's the same error as the webinar list: volume presented as research. Four qualified names beat eleven unqualified ones, and you shouldn't have had to ask twice.

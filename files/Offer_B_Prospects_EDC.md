# Offer B Prospects — Economic Development Councils

You were right that five was too small a sample. Here's the vein I missed.

---

## The category

**Economic Development Councils and regional chambers.** They fit every criterion, and I never looked at them because I was searching for "webinars" instead of thinking about who films people talking.

| Criterion | Why EDCs fit |
|---|---|
| **Talking-head footage** | Their whole content model is interviewing local business leaders — seated, two-camera, lower-thirds |
| **Little social coverage** | 100–200 subscribers, videos in single and double digits |
| **Reachable contact** | Publicly funded or member-funded, so staff directories are on the website by law or convention |
| **Not a media company** | Their product is business attraction, not content |
| **Has budget** | Public funding plus member dues plus named sponsors |

And a bonus nobody else on the list had: **their content features local executives who share it.** Built-in distribution — the guest's own network amplifies every clip.

## How to find the contact — this part is easy now

Unlike every previous prospect list, **EDCs publish their staff.** Public funding and member accountability mean a team page with names, titles, and usually direct emails.

1. Go to the org's site → "Our Team," "Staff," or "About"
2. Find: Marketing Director, Communications Director, Content Manager, or Director of Membership
3. Email is usually listed outright. If not, the pattern is almost always `first.last@` or `finitial+last@`

Two minutes per prospect. Compare to an hour scrubbing webinars.

## The pitch for this category specifically

Don't lead with view counts. Lead with **the guest's network**:

> *"Your Talk CNY episode with Dimitar Karaivanov has real substance in it. Cut into four vertical clips, he'd share them with his network, and so would Community Financial System — which is a much bigger audience than the podcast reaches on its own."*

That reframes clips from "marketing for you" to "distribution through your guests," which is the actual mechanic and something they haven't thought about.

**Second angle, for the sponsored shows:** Excellus is paying for Talk CNY. Sponsors renew on visible impact. More assets per episode means more to show the sponsor at renewal — you're a revenue tool, not a cost.

---

## Honest caveats

**Only two are footage-verified.** Tampa Bay and Chester County. Everything else needs a 30-second thumbnail check before you invest — that's the rule that would have saved five weeks.

**EDCs can be slow.** Public or quasi-public funding often means procurement processes and board approval. Chambers move faster.

**Some may have in-house video.** Tampa Bay's production quality is high enough that someone is being paid to make it — possibly an agency. Ask on the call who produces the series. If it's an agency, the agency is your competitor; if it's a contractor hired per-shoot, editing is likely nobody's job.

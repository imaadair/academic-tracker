# Building the Spec Asset

One clip. Two to three hours the first time, 35 minutes every time after. This is the only thing standing between you and sending the email.

---

## First — something you found

Their YouTube channel: **[youtube.com/@Portlandfilmfestival](https://www.youtube.com/@Portlandfilmfestival)**

151 videos. **329 subscribers.** Most videos in the low hundreds of views. The three most recent are from one to three years ago.

A 16-year-old festival with a Director of Media, eight directors, and a daily slate of panels has a dormant video channel. That isn't a criticism to make to them — it's the evidence that the gap you're pitching into is real, and it should give you confidence that this isn't a solved problem you're intruding on.

**Do not say this in the email.** Nobody wants to hear that their channel is dead. It's for your read on the opportunity, not theirs.

## Step 1 — Find your source footage (30 min)

In order of usefulness:

| Source | What's there |
|---|---|
| [Their YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/@Portlandfilmfestival) | 151 videos including recorded panels. Sort by **Popular** to see what already resonates. |
| [Documentary Filmmaking Panel, 2021](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u2CUOCaRwPQ) | A known full panel — good fallback if browsing stalls |
| [pdxff.com](https://pdxff.com/schedule?viewMode=extended) | 2025 festival site. The Screenwriting Panel and Actors Panel are the flagship talking-head events |
| Instagram @portlandfilm | Shorter clips, and it shows you their current visual brand |

**What you want:** a recorded panel or Q&A with clear audio and a visible speaker. Panels beat screenings — you need someone talking, not a film clip you have no rights to.

**Avoid entirely:** any footage from a submitted film. That's the filmmaker's IP, not PFF's, and cutting it is the one genuine misstep available here.

---

## Step 2 — Find the moment (45 min)

This is the actual skill, and it's where most people cut a bad clip.

Don't scrub the video. **Pull the transcript** — YouTube auto-generates one under the "..." menu → Show transcript. Read it. Reading is five times faster than watching, and it's the same technique the production workflow depends on.

Mark anything that is:

- **A complete thought in 30–50 seconds.** Not a fragment, not a two-minute meander.
- **Opening on a strong declarative sentence.** "Nobody tells you this, but—" or "The biggest mistake I see is—". That sentence becomes your first 1.5 seconds.
- **Specific and actionable.** One concrete idea a filmmaker could use tomorrow.
- **Self-contained.** No references to a film that isn't shown, no inside jokes, no answers to questions the viewer didn't hear.

**The test:** would someone who has never heard of Portland Film Festival find this useful? If no, it's the wrong clip — you've picked something that only works in the room.

Pick three candidates. Cut the best one. The other two become Email 2.

## Step 3 — Build the template (60–90 min, once)

Full spec in `Asset_Template_Spec.md`. The Final Cut order of operations:

**Project setup**

1. New Project → **Custom** settings
2. Video Format: Custom → **1080 × 1920**
3. Rate: match your source (23.98 / 24 / 29.97)

**Reframing 16:9 into 9:16**

Your source is horizontal; your output is vertical. Select the clip → Inspector → Transform → scale up until the speaker fills the frame (usually 180–220%), then reposition so their eyes sit around a third from the top.

If two people are talking, don't try to fit both. Cut between them — scale and reposition on each, and let the cut do the work. Cramming two heads into a vertical frame makes both unreadable.

**Captions**

Recent Final Cut versions have **Transcribe to Captions**, which does the heavy lifting. Two things to watch:

- **They must be burned in.** Sidecar caption tracks don't survive upload to Reels or TikTok. Check the burn-in option in your Share settings; if the styling it produces doesn't match the spec, build a custom Title style instead and use that.
- **Proofread every one.** Auto-transcription mangles names, film titles, and industry terms. A caption reading "Sundance" as "sun dance" is the kind of detail that decides whether you look professional.

This step will eat the most time on your first build. That's expected — it's also why you save it as a reusable style.

**Save everything.** Project preset, caption style, lower third, end card, export preset. The whole point is that asset #2 takes 35 minutes.

---

## Step 4 — Cut it (35 min)

Follow the structure in the spec:

| Time | What |
|---|---|
| 0.0–1.5s | The hook — strongest sentence, plus motion. **No logo, no title card.** |
| 1.5–5s | Lower third: speaker name + role, gone by 5s |
| 5s–end | The substance |
| Final 1.5s | PFF logo + handle, small, corner |

The single most common mistake is opening with a branded intro. Every second before the hook loses viewers. Brand at the end.

Export using the `9x16_Social_Master` preset. Normalize to −14 LUFS.

---

## Step 5 — Deliver it

**Upload unlisted to your own YouTube or Vimeo and send the link.** Don't attach the file — attachments trip spam filters and a 40 MB email is a bad first impression.

**Don't post it publicly anywhere.** It's their footage. You're showing them what you'd do with their material, not distributing it. Say exactly that in the email — *"yours to post, no strings"* — which is already how the draft reads.

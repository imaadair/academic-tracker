# Prospects, v2 — after the HR Cloud dead end

HR Cloud is out. The webinar is entirely screen recording. Two useful things came out of that hour.

---

## The heuristic that would have saved the hour

**A thumbnail that's a title card tells you nothing. A thumbnail that's a frame from the video tells you everything.**

HR Cloud's thumbnail looked ideal — presenter headshot, clean branding, a named speaker. It was a *designed graphic*, not footage. The actual video was 20 minutes of screen recording.

| Thumbnail type | What it tells you | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Designed title card with headshots | Nothing about the footage | Must scrub the video |
| **Actual photo/frame from the event** | **Exactly what you're getting** | **Trust it** |

Plus the keyword filter, which is free:

**Disqualify on sight:** "Product Demo," "Live Demo," "Product Tour," "Walkthrough," "How to use," "Training"
**Qualify on sight:** "Fireside Chat," "Panel," "Q&A," "Interview," "Roundtable," "Conference," "Keynote"

Format words in the title predict the footage better than anything else. Sales-led demos are screen recordings. Conversations are faces.

## The lesson — and a correction

Last round I got excited about conference organisers as a target segment. **That was wrong, for exactly the same structural reason production companies were wrong.**

> **Media companies, ad agencies, event producers, and publishers sell content. So they staff for it.**

### New disqualifier — apply before anything else

If the company's LinkedIn industry is **Advertising, Marketing, Media, Broadcasting, Publishing, or Events** → **skip.** Takes ten seconds and it would have saved this whole detour.

You want companies where **content is a side activity, not the product**: software, professional services, manufacturing, healthcare, associations. They run webinars because marketing says they should, and nobody's actual job is to cut them up.

Scorecard so far:

|---|---|
| Portland Film Festival | No footage, no budget |

| North East Productions | Sells content services |

### Why the client type is better than a SaaS company

A conference organizer isn't one webinar. It's a **library**:

- Every event produces dozens of recorded sessions
- Sponsors need visibility they can point to for renewal
- Clips sell next year's tickets — the content has a direct commercial job
- They're a media company, so content isn't a hard sell internally
- Recurring events mean recurring source material

One SaaS client gives you one webinar a month. One conference organizer gives you thirty sessions per event.

## Both filters, in order

Ten seconds each, applied **before** you watch anything.

**1. Is content their product?** Check the LinkedIn industry tag.
❌ Advertising · Marketing · Media · Broadcasting · Publishing · Events · Production
✅ Software · Professional services · Manufacturing · Healthcare · Associations · Finance

**2. Is the footage talking heads?** Check the title, then the thumbnail.
❌ "Product Demo" · "Live Demo" · "Walkthrough" · "Training" · designed title-card thumbnails
✅ "Fireside Chat" · "Panel" · "Q&A" · "Interview" · "Keynote" · thumbnails that are real photographs

Almost everything dies on one of these two. That's fine — they cost seconds, and they're why the next twenty prospects will go faster than the first six.

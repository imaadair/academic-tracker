# Paid Ideas — Film & Business

**Target: $2,000/month at 10 hrs/week = $50/hour.** Every idea below clears it. Rates verified against current market data, not estimated.

## The five that clear $50/hr

### 1. Film budgets & schedules ⭐

The industry benchmark is roughly **$1,000 per 20 pages of script** for budget development. A 15–20 page short is $750–1,000 for about 8–10 hours of work. A feature runs $3,000–5,000.

**Why you specifically:** your résumé already says *"Orchestrate development, financial planning, and creative resource allocation... including budget management"* as Lead Producer on *Spring, Again*. Plus advanced Excel and a Strategic Management minor. This isn't a stretch — it's the thing you already do, sold.

**Why the demand is reliable:** every grant application requires a budget. Every investor conversation requires a budget. Most first-time filmmakers cannot build one and know it.

| | |
|---|---|
| Rate | $750–1,000 per short, $3,000+ per feature |
| Effective | $75–100/hr |
| To hit $2k | Two shorts a month |
| Tools | Excel is fine for shorts. Movie Magic only if you scale to features. |
| **Résumé value** | **Very high — this is line producing, directly on the EP track** |

### 2. Pitch decks & lookbooks ⭐

Freelance pitch deck work runs **$500–2,000**, with skilled freelancers at **$50–150/hour** and a full deck taking 15–25 hours. Film lookbooks are a narrower niche than startup decks — less competition, and you understand the material in a way a generalist designer doesn't.

**AI does most of the heavy lifting here**, which you've said you want: drafting copy, generating mood references, structuring the narrative. You supply taste and the market read.

| | |
|---|---|
| Rate | $700–1,500 per deck |
| Effective | $80–150/hr |
| To hit $2k | Two to three decks a month |
| **Résumé value** | **High — packaging is development work** |

---

### 3. Grant writing for filmmakers & arts organizations

Film grants are a whole ecosystem — Sundance, ITVS, state film offices, arts councils. **You're in New York, which has real arts funding through NYSCA**, and Syracuse has arts organizations that apply every cycle.

Rate is $50–100/hour or $500–1,500 flat per application. Heavily AI-assisted drafting; the value you add is structure, narrative, and knowing what a panel wants to read.

| | |
|---|---|
| Rate | $500–1,500 per application |
| Effective | $60–100/hr |
| **Résumé value** | **Medium-high — producers raise money** |

### 4. Actor & director reel editing

Every working actor needs a current reel and most hate making them. $200–500 per reel, 3–5 hours in Final Cut.

**The easiest clients to find in this entire document.** Actors are numerous, visible, and used to paying for career materials. Low sales friction.

| | |
|---|---|
| Rate | $200–500 per reel |
| Effective | $60–100/hr |
| To hit $2k | Eight reels a month |
| Résumé value | Low-medium — but the cash is the most reliable here |

## What I'd actually build: bundle 1, 2 and 3

Here's the thing that makes this work — **budgets, lookbooks, and grant applications are the same client.**

A filmmaker trying to get a project financed needs all three, usually at the same time, and usually has no idea how to produce any of them. Selling them separately means three sales. Selling them together means one.

> ### The Financing Package — $2,500
> Budget · schedule · lookbook · one grant application, tailored
>
> *"Everything you need to ask someone for money."*

**One project a month hits your target**, at roughly 25 hours. That's inside 10 hrs/week with room for sales.

And the résumé line writes itself:

> *Developed budgets, schedules, and financing materials for [N] independent film projects, supporting $[X] in grant and investor submissions.*

That is **producer work**. Not adjacent to your Executive Producer goal — literally the first rung of it. It's the only idea in this entire conversation where the money and the career point in exactly the same direction, and it doesn't depend on raw material you have to go hunting for. The input is the client's script, and a filmmaker without a script isn't a client.

## Where the clients are

This is the part that killed every previous attempt, so be honest about it upfront. But filmmakers seeking financing are *visibly* seeking financing, which is a real advantage over "companies with talking-head footage."

| Channel | Why |
|---|---|
| **Portland Film Festival alumni network** | You're a past winner — a genuine warm door, and a far better use of that relationship than selling them video services |
| **Film Independent / IFP communities** | Filmmakers actively packaging projects |
| **Stage 32** | Purpose-built for exactly this, with a lounge for financing questions |
| **Grant deadline calendars** | Reverse-engineer it — anyone applying to a grant with a March deadline needs a budget in January |
| **Newhouse & VPA students** | Thesis films need budgets, and their parents pay |
| **Local film offices** | Oregon Film and the NY state offices maintain filmmaker lists |

That last channel is the sharpest: **grant deadlines tell you exactly when people need you.** No other business in this conversation had a built-in timing signal.

## Test it before building it

The lesson from the last five attempts: **validate before investing.**

Post once in a filmmaker community offering to build **one free budget** in exchange for a testimonial. If three people reply, the demand is real. If nobody does, you've learned it in a week instead of five.

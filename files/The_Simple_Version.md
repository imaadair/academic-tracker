# The Simple Version

$500–1,000/month at 10 hrs/week = **$12–25/hour**.

You don't need a business for that. You need paid work — which is a solved problem, takes no sales, and has no supply risk.

---

## Why this is the right call

Every model we tried needed you to be a founder: find clients, build materials, do outreach, wait on replies. Six weeks of that produced nothing, and it wasn't for lack of effort — the constraint set had no solution in it.

Meanwhile the things that actually move your career have been sitting still. **Austin Film Festival screeners are open right now. Sundance opened Aug 31. Sundance Ignite is February. *Spring, Again* is unfinished.**

Dropping the money target doesn't mean giving up. It means putting your ten hours where they compound.

---

## The base: campus work — $400–700/month

**Reliable, zero sales, on your résumé, and film-adjacent if you pick right.**

| Where | Rate | Why it's good |
|---|---|---|
| **Expand your RA work** | $15–20/hr | You already do this for Dr. Murphy. Ask for more hours, or ask her to refer you to another faculty member. *Research Assistant* is a real line, and you have a champion. |
| **Newhouse facilities / studios / equipment desk** | $15–18/hr | Film-adjacent, on campus, flexible around class. You're already in the building. |
| **SU research centers** | $15–20/hr | Maxwell and iSchool both hire undergrads |
| **Tutoring through SU** | $20–40/hr | Highest campus rate; your 3.7 qualifies you |

**15 hours a month at $17 is roughly $255. Thirty hours is $510.** No clients, no pitching, no waiting on email.

**Start with Dr. Murphy.** She already knows your work, and "do you have more hours, or know anyone who needs someone?" is a two-minute conversation with a high hit rate. That's the single easiest dollar available to you.

---

## The boost: film school admissions consulting — $500–1,000 in season

This gets much better at the lower target. At $2,000/month you needed a steady client flow. At $500–1,000 you need **one or two students, once.**

- Rate: $100–150/hr is defensible for you
- 5–10 hours a month = $500–1,500
- Season: **November through January**, overlapping winter break
- You got into Newhouse, did USC and NYU Paris, and won a festival competition as a teenager. Almost no consultant can review a creative supplement or a director's reel. You can.

**Finding one client is genuinely easy:** your own high school, family friends, r/ApplyingToCollege, local arts high schools. You need *one*.

## The optional third: reel editing — $200–500 each

If you want film-adjacent freelance without building a company. Actors need current reels, hate making them, and are the easiest clients in film to reach. Three to five hours each in Final Cut.

Two a month is $400–1,000. No prospecting infrastructure required — post once in a local actors' group.

## One honest note about the blueprint

Your $10M-by-30 plan has you at **$2,500–4,500/month at age 20**. That number is where all the pressure in this conversation came from, and it's worth knowing it's aggressive to the point of being the reason six models felt like failures.

Most people who reach that trajectory don't do it through undergraduate side income. They do it by landing well after graduating, then compounding. **The Summer 2027 internship is worth more to that plan than any amount you'd earn this year** — which makes the applications, not the side hustle, the highest-leverage thing in front of you.

The plan isn't wrong. The Age 20 row is.

# Offer B, Retargeted: B2B Webinar Repurposing

**Replaces the Portland Film Festival plan.** Same offer, same pricing structure, same delivery pipeline — different market.

---

## What went wrong, briefly

Your blueprint said "mid-sized corporate marketing teams." I narrowed it to film festivals and indie production companies for résumé value. That segment fails on the two things this business needs:

| | Footage exists? | No in-house editor? | Has budget? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Portland Film Festival | ✗ nothing usable | ✓ | ✗ volunteer nonprofit |
| Desert Island Studios | ✓ | ✗ **sells this service** | ✓ |
| North East Productions | ✓ | ✗ probably | ✓ |

Production companies *are* editors — pitching them editing is backwards. Volunteer festivals have neither footage nor money. You spent a week finding that out, which is cheap compared to finding out in September.

## The new target

**Companies that already publish long-form video and have nobody cutting it up.**

Three qualifiers, all of which must be true:

1. **They already produce recorded video** — webinars, recorded panels, podcasts, conference sessions, product demos
2. **No in-house video editor** — marketing team, but no video person
3. **Real marketing budget** — not a nonprofit running on volunteers

### The searchable signal

This is the operational unlock, and it's the thing PFF failed:

> **A company YouTube channel full of 45-minute webinars and zero vertical clips.**

You can *see* that from the outside, for free, in about ninety seconds per company. No guessing whether the footage exists — you're looking straight at it. And it doubles as the spec asset source, because it's already public.

### Where to look, in order

| Segment | Why it works | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| **B2B SaaS / tech** | Constant webinars, marketing budget, fast decisions, remote-native | Some have in-house content teams |
| **Professional services** — law, accounting, consulting | Thought-leadership video, serious money, famously bad at social | Slow decisions, conservative |
| **Trade & professional associations** | Conference sessions recorded every year, dues revenue, need member engagement | Committee approvals |
| **Higher ed programs & healthcare systems** | Enormous recorded output; locally, Upstate Medical and SU are the two biggest employers in the region | Slow procurement; HIPAA in clinical settings |

Start with B2B SaaS. Fastest yes, cleanest fit, and the footage is always public.

## The tailwind — and your cold-email hook

Industry coverage reports that YouTube's late-2025 algorithm changes sharply reduced long-form discovery slots while Shorts now drive roughly 200 billion daily views. Treat the specific numbers as marketing-blog figures rather than gospel — but the direction is not in dispute, and B2B marketers feel it.

The pitch math, which is genuinely compelling:

> A 45-minute webinar watched live by 200 people, cut into 10 captioned vertical clips, can reach ten thousand.

That's the whole argument in one sentence. Their webinar library is a sunk cost producing almost no return, and one long-form recording yields 15–25 short clips.

---

## Pricing — an honest correction

Market reality, from current service providers:

- **$350** for 10 clips from one long-form video (5-day turnaround) is a going project rate
- **$500–$2,000/month** is the typical B2B budget for repurposing handled by a freelancer or subscription

**Your blueprint's $2,500/month sits at or above the top of that range.** That's achievable with a track record; it's a hard first sale with none.

### Revised ladder

| Stage | Price | Deliverable |
|---|---|---|
| **Pilot** | $500 | 10 clips from one existing webinar |
| **Starter retainer** | $1,500/mo | 16 clips + captions + distribution calendar |
| **Full retainer** | $2,500/mo | Above + multi-platform scheduling + monthly reporting |

**Two clients at $1,500 clears your $2,000 target at roughly 7 hrs/week.** Get there, then raise. Don't lead at $2,500 with an empty portfolio — you'll lose deals you could have won and learned from.

This market is more competitive than film festivals. That's the trade you made: easier to sell into, more people already selling. Your edge is turnaround speed and being an actual filmmaker rather than a template farm — lean on both.

## Finding 20 prospects in an hour

1. Search YouTube for `"webinar" [industry]` — filter to the last year
2. Open the channel. Does it have long-form webinars and **no** Shorts tab activity?
3. If yes, it's a prospect. Log: company, channel URL, best webinar, marketing contact
4. Find the contact on LinkedIn — Marketing Manager, Content Marketing, Demand Gen. Avoid the CMO at large companies; you want the person who owns the calendar
5. Repeat

Twenty prospects gets you roughly one pilot at normal cold-outreach rates. Build the list before you write a single email.

## The demo asset — now trivial

The problem that killed PFF disappears here. Every prospect has public webinar footage.

**Build one strong demo first** from any well-produced public webinar or conference talk. That's your portfolio piece, reusable with everyone.

**Then, for your top five prospects only,** cut a clip from *their* footage. That's your blueprint's Audit & Prototype method, and it works properly here because the raw material is guaranteed to exist.

Everything in `Asset_Template_Spec.md` and `Spec_Asset_Build_Guide.md` still applies — only the source changes.

## The résumé line, honestly

You traded some industry-specific résumé value for a business that actually works. Here's what survives, and it isn't nothing:

> **Founder & Principal, [Company]** | 2026–Present
> Built and operate a B2B video operations business serving [N] corporate clients. Produce 30+ short-form assets monthly from client webinar libraries, managing the full intake-to-publish workflow. Scaled from cold outreach to $[X],000 in monthly recurring revenue while enrolled full-time.

For **UTA, CAA, and WME** this is close to ideal — agencies hire for hustle, client management, and revenue instinct, and this proves all three better than a festival credit would have. For **A24 and Blumhouse development roles** it's weaker, and your production credits, script coverage, and the Guinevere Turner writers' room carry those applications. Those are already strong.

Money here, development credentials there. That's a clean division, and it's better than a business that doesn't work.

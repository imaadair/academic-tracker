# Building the BHS Sponsorship Kit

---

## ⚠ Step 0 — Clear it with Student Engagement first

Syracuse has a **Student Fundraising and Promotions Policy**. Recognized Student Organizations must file an **Event Request Form on CUSE Activities** and get approval from **Student Engagement** before fundraising or soliciting.

The policy also says activity must not violate *"legal, tax, or corporate restraints upon the University"* — which usually means SU has exclusive corporate partnerships a student org can't cut across.

**The penalty for getting this wrong is a freeze on future RSO activities.** You're Secretary. That would land on BHS and on you.

**Do this before anything else:** email Student Engagement and ask two questions.

> 1. What's the approval process for an RSO seeking corporate sponsorship?
> 2. Are there companies or categories we're restricted from approaching because of existing University agreements?

Fifteen minutes, and it's the difference between a portfolio piece and a problem. If the answer is "route everything through Advancement," that's still fine — the kit is just as useful, it goes through a different door.

---

## What BHS is actually selling

Worth being clear about this, because it determines every number in the deck.

**Companies are not buying event visibility. They're buying recruiting access to high-achieving Black students at a top-30 university.**

That's a market with real money in it — corporate campus recruiting and DEI budgets. Your members are a pool that Fortune 500 recruiting teams spend heavily to reach, and BHS is a filter that has already done the sorting: honors program, high GPA, leadership.

Frame the deck around that and the numbers hold up. Frame it around "banner at our event" and it's worth $250.

---

## Benchmark pricing

The closest comparable is **NSBE** — a Black student professional organisation with mature corporate sponsorship. Their university chapter tiers:

| Tier | Range |
|---|---|
| Bronze | $1,000–2,000 |
| Silver | $2,000–5,000 |
| Gold | $5,000+ |

Professional chapters run $2,500–15,000. Corporate sustainability partners pay for exhibit and interview booths at $2,500–3,500 each.

**That's your defensible pricing floor.** When someone asks why a tier costs $2,500, the answer is that it's what comparable organisations charge — not a number you invented.

## What I need from you

Ten items. Most you'll know off the top of your head; a couple need a look at records.

### Audience
1. **Total active members**
2. **Class year breakdown** — juniors and seniors matter most to recruiters
3. **Schools/majors represented** — Whitman, Newhouse, Engineering, Maxwell, iSchool. This determines *which* companies care.
4. **GPA threshold or honors requirement** for membership
5. **Email list size** and typical open rate if you have it
6. **Instagram/social following**

### Events
7. **Annual event calendar** — what you run, roughly when
8. **Typical attendance** per event type, plus your biggest event

### Existing state
9. **Current funding** — SU allocation, dues, anything else. And the annual budget number.
10. **Any existing corporate relationships**, even informal ones

Plus, if it exists: **logo files and any brand colours**.

## One thing to decide with your board

Sponsorship money for a student org can create obligations — logo placement, speaking slots, access to member contact information. **Member data is the one to be careful about.** Selling recruiting access is legitimate; handing over a member list without consent is not, and it's the kind of thing that damages trust in an organisation you're an officer of.

Build the tiers so that recruiting access means *presence at events and a résumé book members opt into* — never a list scraped from your roster.

# The Operating Flow

How the whole thing runs, start to finish. Every other file is a component; this is the wiring.

---

## The funnel

```
[SETUP]  one time, ~3 hrs
   │  FCP template + demo clip + payment method
   ▼
[PROSPECT]  1 hr/week ──────────► 20 qualified companies
   │  YouTube search → 90-sec qualify → log
   ▼
[OUTREACH]  2 hrs/week ─────────► 5 emails/week
   │  Tier A: custom clip (5 companies)
   │  Tier B: demo clip (15 companies)
   │  One follow-up at day 5
   ▼
[CONVERSATION]  ~20% reply ─────► 15-min call
   │  Qualify: footage? owner? budget?
   ▼
[PILOT]  $500 ──────────────────► 10 clips, ~6 hrs
   │  One webinar → transcribe → cut → deliver
   ▼
[CONVERT]  wrap call + numbers ─► $1,500/mo retainer
   │
   ▼
[DELIVER]  ~13 hrs/month/client
   │  Monthly: intake → 16 assets → report
   ▼
[CEILING]  2 clients = $3,000/mo at ~7 hrs/week
```

**Roughly 20 prospects → 4 replies → 2 calls → 1 pilot → 1 retainer.** Plan for those ratios and nothing surprises you.

## Stage 0 — Setup (this week, ~3 hrs)

| Task | Time | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Build the Final Cut template | 90 min | Reusable project, caption style, export preset |
| Cut the demo from a conference talk | 35 min | One portfolio clip, unlisted link |
| Payment: Wave or Stripe invoicing | 20 min | A way to get paid |
| Google Drive folder structure | 15 min | `/ClientName/Raw`, `/Delivered`, `/Brand` |

**No website needed yet.** A demo clip and a clear email do the work. Build a site after the first client pays, not before — it's the most popular way to feel productive without doing anything.

## Stage 1 — Prospect (1 hr/week)

```
YouTube search by vertical
   → open channel
   → recent webinars? Shorts empty? 50–500 employees? no in-house video?
   → log to tracker + note best webinar
```

Batch it. One focused hour produces more than seven scattered ten-minute sessions, and switching cost is what kills small time budgets.

File: `Prospect_List.md`

## Stage 2 — Outreach (2 hrs/week)

Two tiers, because custom clips don't scale but they convert far better.

| | Who | What you send | Time each |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tier A** | Top 5 prospects | Clip cut from **their** webinar | 35 min |
| **Tier B** | Everyone else | Your demo clip | 5 min |

**Send 5 per week — 1 Tier A, 4 Tier B.** One follow-up at day 5, then stop. Two follow-ups is persistence; three is a nuisance.

Log every send. Without a log you will lose track by week three, follow up twice on the same person, and look disorganized to exactly the audience you're selling organization to.

File: `B2B_Target_Plan.md` (email copy)

## Stage 3 — Conversation

When someone replies, the call has one job: **find out whether this deal can physically work.**

Ask in this order:

1. How often do you run webinars, and where do the recordings live?
2. Who currently touches them after the event?
3. What are you trying to get out of the clips — reach, leads, sales enablement?
4. Do you have brand guidelines and a logo file?
5. Is there budget for this, or would it need to get approved somewhere?

Question 5 is the one people skip because it's uncomfortable. Ask it anyway. A no here saves you six hours of pilot work.

Then send the pilot scope the same day, while the call is still warm. Same-day is a small thing that signals exactly what you're selling.

## Stage 4 — Pilot ($500, ~6 hrs)

```
Day 1     Intake: webinar file, logo, fonts, handles
Day 2     Transcribe + select 10 moments
Day 3-4   Cut, caption, brand
Day 5     Deliver all 10 + one-page notes
Day 8     One consolidated revision round
Day 10    Final delivery
```

**One revision round, batched.** Say it in writing before you start. Unlimited revisions is the single most reliable way to turn a profitable project into a losing one.

Over-deliver on count, not on scope: send 11 clips instead of 10. Cheap for you, memorable for them, and it doesn't set a precedent for extra work.

---

## Stage 5 — Convert

Two weeks after delivery, ask for the numbers: views, engagement, any inbound. Put them on one page next to what you delivered.

Then: *"Want me to keep doing this every month?"*

**Convert on evidence, not enthusiasm.** If the clips performed, the page makes the argument for you. If they didn't, find out why before you pitch a retainer you'll struggle to renew.

File: `PFF_Pilot_Proposal.md` — the structure ports directly; swap festival language for webinar language.

## Stage 6 — Deliver the retainer (~13 hrs/month)

```
Week 1   Intake — client drops the month's recordings
Week 2   Produce 8 assets
Week 3   Produce 8 assets + deliver batch
Week 4   Schedule, report, plan next month
```

Running AI-first (see `AI_Workflow.md`):

| Task | Hrs/month |
|---|---|
| Upload + generate | 0.5 |
| **Review and select clips** | 1.0 |
| Caption corrections + fixes | 1.5 |
| Intake + client review cycles | 1.0 |
| Scheduling + reporting | 1.0 |
| **Total** | **~5 hrs** |

## Weekly rhythm

| Phase | Delivery | Outreach | Admin | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Pre-client** | 0 | 3 hrs | 0.5 | **3.5 hrs** |
| **1 client** | 1.25 | 3 hrs | 1 | **5.25 hrs** |
| **3 clients** | 3.75 | 2 hrs | 1 | **6.75 hrs** |
| **5 clients** | 6.25 | 0.5 | 1.5 | **8.25 hrs** |

**The ceiling is roughly 5 clients — about $7,500/month at 8 hrs/week.** That's the AI-first number. Doing it by hand in Final Cut, the ceiling was 2 clients and $3,000.

You won't get near five soon, and you shouldn't try. But it means the constraint on this business stops being your hours and starts being your sales pipeline — which is a far better problem.

## Academic overlay

| Date | What | Effect on the business |
|---|---|---|
| **Aug 24** | Classes start | Front-load outreach before this |
| Sept 7 | Labor Day | — |
| **Oct 12–13** | Fall break | Light week; use it to catch up |
| **Oct 14** | Midterm | **No new client starts this week** |
| Nov 22–29 | Thanksgiving | Clients slow down too — good delivery week |
| **Dec 8–15** | Last classes + finals | **No new clients. Deliver only.** |

**Sell August–September. Deliver October. Hold steady through December.** Don't sign anyone new in the two weeks before midterms or finals — the fastest way to lose a client is to miss the first deadline.

## Decision gates

Check these honestly rather than grinding.

| Point | If this happens | Do this |
|---|---|---|
| 20 emails sent | 0 replies | The clip or the email is wrong. Rewrite before sending 20 more. |
| 5 calls held | 0 pilots | Price or offer is wrong. Try $350 for 6 clips. |
| 1 pilot delivered | No conversion | Ask why, directly. Usually delivery quality or the wrong buyer. |
| 2 pilots, 0 conversions | — | The market's fine; something in your delivery isn't. Come back and we'll diagnose. |
| First retainer signed | — | **Stop selling for one month.** Nail delivery before adding load. |

That last one matters most. The most common way this fails isn't too few clients — it's signing a second before the first is running smoothly, then doing both badly.

# The $10K Job

**Target: $833/month.** What matters isn't the rate — it's hours, because you're already at 20 a week.

| Rate | Hours/week needed |
|---|---|
| $20/hr (more RA, agency tutoring) | **10 hrs** |
| $35/hr | 6 hrs |
| **$50/hr** | **4 hrs** |
| $60/hr | 3.2 hrs |
| $150/hr (admissions consulting) | 1.4 hrs |

Ten extra hours a week on top of 20 hours of RA work, a full course load, two exec boards, and a film is where the risk lives. **Four hours isn't.** So the whole question is how to get your rate above $50.

---

## The answer: private test prep tutoring

Current market: **entry-level and student tutors charge $45–80/hr** privately. Experienced independents get $80–200. Agency jobs pay $20–25 — that's the number to avoid.

**You can credibly charge $50–60.** You're a 3.7 student at a top-tier program who got into Newhouse, USC's summer program, and NYU Paris. For a parent in DeWitt paying for their kid's SAT prep, that's exactly the profile they're buying.

**At $55/hr you need three and a half hours a week.** Two students, one session each.

### Why this beats the alternatives

| Option | Rate | Hrs/wk | Problem |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Private test prep** | $50–60 | **3.5** | Have to find clients |
| More RA hours | $20 | 10 | May not be available; 30 hrs/wk total |
| Agency tutoring (Varsity, Kaplan) | $20–25 | 9 | They find clients, you take the cut |
| Campus job | $15–18 | 12 | Too many hours for the money |
| Admissions consulting | $100–150 | 1.4 | Nov–Jan only — a supplement, not a base |

## Where the clients are

Syracuse is unusually good for this: a college town wrapped in affluent suburbs with competitive high schools.

**Target the districts, not the city:**
- Jamesville–DeWitt
- Fayetteville–Manlius
- Skaneateles
- Christian Brothers Academy / Manlius Pebble Hill (private, high college pressure)

**How to reach parents, in order of speed:**

1. **Start on Wyzant.** They supply the clients, you set your rate, they take a cut. Slower money but a first client in one to two weeks and reviews you keep. Use it to build a rating, then move referrals off-platform.
2. **Local parent Facebook groups** for those districts. One post, honest and short.
3. **Your own high school.** Counselors keep tutor lists and respond to alumni.
4. **Referrals.** One happy parent in a competitive district produces two more. This is how the practice actually fills.

**Ramp:** first client in two to four weeks, two to three clients by week six to eight. Budget for that.

---

## The stack that makes this worth more

Test prep and film school admissions consulting are **the same customer.**

A junior doing SAT prep has parents already thinking about applications. If that kid has any interest in film, media, or communications, you are not a tutor anymore — you're someone who got into the exact programs they're targeting.

So: **lead with test prep at $55**, and when the conversation turns to applications, that's a separate engagement at $100–150/hr. Same family, no new client acquisition.

That's how 3.5 hours a week turns into more than $833 without adding hours.

## One honest note on the trade

You're solving for $10,000 of net worth at 20. Compounding at 15%, that's roughly $40,000 by 30 — real, but not what decides the plan.

Three and a half hours a week is a fine price for it. Ten would not be, because those hours would come out of the screening committees, the Ignite application, and finishing *Spring, Again* — and a Summer 2027 seat at A24 or NBCU is worth considerably more to the $10M line than $40,000 is.

Get the rate up, keep the hours down. That's the whole play.

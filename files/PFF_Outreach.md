# Portland Film Festival — Outreach Sequence

---

## The timing is unusually good, and it's the whole play

The 2026 festival runs **Wednesday September 30 – Sunday October 4** — 53 days, or about 7.5 weeks, from today (Aug 8).

That means two things.

**Their pre-festival marketing is probably already locked.** Pitching to fix their August promo is pitching into a plan they've already made.

**Their during- and post-festival content is almost certainly not staffed.** Five days of panels, Q&As, and filmmaker interviews generate an enormous volume of footage, and small festivals have no capacity to cut it while the festival is happening. Most of it never gets used. The team is exhausted by day three and the material goes cold within two weeks.

So don't pitch "social media help." Pitch **festival coverage** — the specific thing they need in seven weeks and cannot staff.

One more lever: their sponsors include Comcast, Koerner Camera, and the Native Arts + Cultures Foundation. Sponsors need visibility they can point to. A festival that hands sponsors a folder of branded vertical assets renews sponsorships more easily. Say this out loud in the pilot conversation — it reframes you from a cost into a revenue tool.

**Send this week.** Past early September they're in survival mode and won't onboard anyone new.

---

## "Don't they already have someone?" — answered

You checked. The answer is no, and it's more lopsided than I'd argued.

**~10,000 Instagram followers. Nothing posted since last October.** Ten months dark. Coverage of the entire 2025 festival amounted to some photos, one graphic, and one video — for a five-day event with daily panels, classes, and pitch sessions.

Three things follow.

**The organic account is unowned.** A ten-month gap isn't a busy team behind schedule. It's nobody's job right now.

**Paid traffic is landing on a dead profile.** You noticed ads running for this year's festival. So someone is buying reach to an account whose feed stops in October 2025. Everyone who clicks through to the profile sees an abandoned page. That's a live, nameable problem — money going out, and the destination undermining it.

**Submissions have been open since July 26 and they haven't said a word.** For a 501(c)(3), submission fees are revenue, and their deadlines ladder upward — Sept 9 discount, Sept 16 early bird, Sept 30 advance, with fees rising at each step. Ten thousand followers who care about independent film, in the middle of the revenue season, hearing nothing.

That last one matters for a reason beyond the pitch: **the clip you're cutting is perfect submission-season content.** Filmmakers giving advice to filmmakers is exactly what attracts more filmmakers. You're not just showing him a sample — you're showing him something he could post tomorrow that serves a deadline three weeks out.

---

## The risk this creates

An account dark for ten months with 10K followers usually means one of three things: nobody owns it, nobody has budget, or leadership doesn't rate it. Only the first is good news for you.

**A visible gap is not the same as willingness to pay.** This is the classic trap in cold outreach — "they obviously need this" and "they will buy this" are different claims. A volunteer-run nonprofit with eight directors may genuinely not have $750 in discretionary spend, and "Director of Media" at a festival this size is quite possibly an unpaid role.

Two ways to prepare:

**Ask why on the call.** *"Is social something you've been wanting to get back to, or is it just not a priority right now?"* His answer tells you whether there's budget or just a hole.

**Have a scope-down ready — not a discount.** If $750 is impossible, offer **6 assets for $350** rather than 12 for less. Reducing scope protects your rate; cutting price sets it permanently. And still don't work free: a volunteer doesn't convert to a retainer.

---

### The email survives either answer

It leads with a free asset and a soft ask, and Email 2 says outright: *"If festival coverage is already handled, no problem at all — just tell me."* Nothing is staked on the gap. And question one on the call — *are you recording panels this year, and who's cutting them?* — settles in thirty seconds what no amount of outside research can.

**Still don't tell him the account is dead.** He knows. Arriving with finished work at the exact moment he needs it makes the point without anyone having to say it.

---

## Before you send

1. **Cut the spec asset first.** The email is worthless without it. Pull a 2025 panel or Q&A off their YouTube or Instagram, cut one 45-second vertical using the template spec. Attach it, or better, upload unlisted and link — attachments trip spam filters.
2. **Check your Future Filmmakers year and placement** so you state it accurately.

## Email 1 — the opener

**Subject line options:**
- `Cut you a vertical from last year's panel`
- `Future Filmmakers alum — made you something for October`
- `A festival clip, no strings`

> Hi Ben,
>
> I'm Ima Kennerly — I won Future Filmmakers a few years back, and I'm at Newhouse now studying Television, Radio and Film.
>
> With the festival about seven weeks out, I took one of last year's [panel/Q&A] clips and cut it into a vertical asset — captions burned in, reframed for Reels and LinkedIn. It's here: [link]. Yours to post, no strings.
>
> I did it because you run at least two classes, panels, or pitch sessions every single day of the festival, and that's more good footage than any team has time to cut. Most of it goes cold. Since submissions are open, this one might be useful now — filmmakers giving advice tends to bring in more filmmakers.
>
> If it'd help to have someone turning footage around during the run this year, I'd be glad to talk. Either way, I can send a couple more.
>
> Ima Kennerly
> [phone] · [email]

**Why it's built this way**

The Future Filmmakers line is the entire reason this email gets opened — it makes you someone their program produced, not a vendor. State it once and move on; leaning on it harder makes it a claim rather than a fact.

The asset goes in with no conditions. Your blueprint's §1.3 gets this right: a finished clip is proof, and proof beats a pitch when you have no track record.

Naming the real problem — *good footage goes cold* — shows you understand festival operations. That sentence is what separates this from every other student email they get.

The ask is deliberately soft. You're seven weeks from their busiest period; the goal is a conversation, not a close.

---

## Email 2 — follow-up (send 5 days later if no reply)

Most cold outreach dies from not following up. One follow-up roughly doubles response. Send exactly one.

> Hi Ben — following up briefly.
>
> I cut two more from the same session: [link].
>
> If festival coverage is already handled, no problem at all — just tell me and I'll stop filling your inbox. If it isn't, a 15-minute call in the next couple of weeks would be enough to sort out whether this is worth doing.
>
> Ima

Giving them an easy out is what makes this land as helpful rather than pushy. It also gets you a real answer instead of silence.

## The pilot offer (for the call)

Adapted from your blueprint's §1.3, Step 5 — reshaped around festival week.

**Festival Coverage Pilot — $750**

| | |
|---|---|
| Window | Festival week + 7 days (through Oct 11) |
| Deliverable | 12 vertical assets, captioned, brand-consistent |
| Turnaround | 24 hours on 4 of them (same-day social while the festival is live) |
| Includes | 2 sponsor-attributed assets |
| Review | One consolidated round of notes |

The 24-hour turnaround on a subset is what makes this valuable. Anyone can cut clips in November. Same-day content while the festival is running is the thing they cannot do themselves, and it's what you'll be remembered for.

**The conversion:** at the wrap conversation, bring a one-page summary — assets delivered, views, sponsor mentions — and offer the $2,500/month retainer for year-round programming, submissions pushes, and Future Filmmakers content. Convert on evidence, not on enthusiasm.

---

## Two things not to do

**Don't discount below $750.** You'll be tempted to say "free, for the experience." Don't. A free pilot makes you a volunteer, and volunteers don't convert to retainers — they become people who get asked for more free work. $750 is already generous for festival-week turnaround.

**Don't lead with your résumé.** They don't need your credits; they need someone reliable in seven weeks. One line of context, then straight to what you made for them.

---

## If they say no

Run the same sequence at **Desert Island Studios** and **North East Productions** — both warm, both Portland, both in your résumé already. The spec asset and template carry over; only the footage changes. The Oregon Film Office is a good fourth.

A no from PFF costs you one email and teaches you what the objection actually is. Ask for it: *"Totally understand — out of curiosity, is it budget or is coverage already handled?"* That answer is worth more than the pilot.

## Read the view counts correctly

A webinar with 17 views does **not** automatically mean a company that knows it has a problem.

B2B webinars usually earn their keep at *registration* — the live event captures leads, and the YouTube upload is an afterthought nobody is measuring. Plenty of marketers genuinely don't care about that number.

Which means the pitch is **not** "your views are terrible." That's insulting and it argues against a metric they may not track.

The pitch is: **"You already paid to produce this. Here's more return on it."** Same content, more surface area, no new production cost. That works whether or not they care about YouTube — because the clips go to LinkedIn, where B2B buyers actually are.

## The method — build 20 in an hour

### 1. Search YouTube

Sort by upload date, filter to the last month. These strings produced the list above:

```
[industry] webinar
"webinar" [problem area] software
webinar recording 2026 [category]
[sector] compliance webinar
```

Swap in verticals: HR tech, cybersecurity, fintech, legal tech, healthcare IT, supply chain, insurance, martech, EdTech.

### 2. Qualify — 90 seconds per company

| Check | Pass |
|---|---|
| Long-form webinars posted in the last 3 months? | ✓ ongoing program |
| Shorts tab empty or stale? | ✓ nobody repurposing |
| **Presenter visible on camera, not pure screen-share?** | ✓ **see below** |
| Company site looks funded — real product, real team page? | ✓ has budget |
| Any sign of an in-house video/content producer? | ✗ disqualify |
| Employee count roughly 50–500? | ✓ sweet spot |

### The on-camera check matters most

AI clipping tools run about 90% accurate on single-speaker talking-head footage and fall apart on screen-share-heavy recordings where there's no face to frame. A 45-minute slide deck with voiceover is bad for the tools *and* bad for vertical social — you can't build a compelling 9:16 clip out of slides.

- ✅ Webcam or studio presenter visible most of the runtime
- ✅ Two-person interview with speaker switching
- ⚠️ Presenter in a corner, slides dominant — workable, more manual effort
- ❌ Pure screen-share with voiceover — **skip**

Ninety seconds to check, and it saves you from selling something you can't deliver well.

Under 50 employees usually means no budget. Over 500 usually means an in-house team or a procurement process.

### 3. Find the contact

LinkedIn, in this order of preference:

1. Content Marketing Manager
2. Demand Generation Manager
3. Marketing Manager
4. Head of Marketing *(only at smaller companies)*

**Avoid the CMO** at anything sizeable — you want whoever owns the content calendar and feels the pain, not the person who'll forward your email into a void.

Email patterns are usually `first@`, `first.last@`, or `firstinitiallast@`. Check the pattern on their site's contact or press page.

## The demo asset — use conference footage, not a prospect webinar

Don't build your portfolio piece from a 17-view webinar. Those are Zoom recordings with laptop audio and screen-share slides — you'd be showing your best work on your worst possible source.

**Use a well-produced conference talk instead.** Free, public, professionally lit and mic'd, and in exactly the business genre you're selling into:

- **SaaStr Annual** — B2B SaaS, huge library on YouTube
- **HubSpot INBOUND** — marketing, well produced
- **Web Summit / TNW** — business and tech keynotes
- **Google I/O or AWS re:Invent breakout sessions** — technical but immaculately shot
- **TED / TEDx business talks** — the gold standard for single-speaker framing

Pick a talk where one person is on camera in a medium shot. That reframes to 9:16 cleanly and gives you a demo that looks like the output you're promising.

**Then, for your top five prospects only,** cut a clip from their actual footage — rough source and all. That contrast is fine: the polished demo proves your ceiling, the custom clip proves you did the homework.

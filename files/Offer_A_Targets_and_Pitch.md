Real organisations, verified as active and sponsorship-dependent.

## The offer

### Sponsorship Kit — $1,200

| Deliverable | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Sponsorship deck** | 3–4 tiered packages, designed, ready to send |
| **Audience one-pager** | Reach, demographics, attendance — the numbers a sponsor asks for first |
| **Tier pricing rationale** | What each level is worth and why, so they can defend the number |
| **Corporate membership framework** | Recurring-revenue structure, where it applies |
| **25 named sponsor targets** | Company, contact name, title, why they fit |

**Optional:** 15% commission on anything closed within 12 months from the provided list.

### Why the last item is the product

Anyone can design a deck. A $400 freelancer will. **The prospect list is what makes this $1,200** — knowing *who to send it to* is the part organisations genuinely can't do, and it's the same research work you've been doing all week.

Lead with it. It's your differentiator and it's the thing that sounds like work they'd never get around to.

### Smaller entry point — $350

**Sponsorship Audit + Prospect List.** No deck. You review what they currently send sponsors, tell them what's missing, and hand over 25 named targets. Use this when someone balks at $1,200 — it's a foot in the door, not a discount.

## Tier 1 — best paid targets

### ⭐ Syracuse International Film Festival
`syracusefilmfest.com` · 122 Clarence Drive, Syracuse NY

Works with the **Onondaga County Film Commission**. Runs film-based programming year-round, not just an annual festival — which means recurring sponsorship needs rather than one cycle. They already advertise "partnership opportunities," which means they're selling sponsorship and know they need to.

**Why you specifically:** Newhouse TRF student, local, and a **first-place winner at a film festival's emerging filmmaker competition**. You speak festival, which almost no sponsorship freelancer does.

**Best single target on this list.**

### CNY Arts, Inc.
`cnyarts.org`

The regional arts council — runs the Syracuse Art Trail and maintains the CNY events calendar. Two possibilities: they're a client themselves, and they're a **directory of every arts organisation in the region**, most of which need exactly this.

Even if they don't buy, one conversation gives you your entire Tier 2 list.

### Festival of Music
`festival-of-music.org`

CNY music festival with an existing sponsors page and an open invitation to sponsors. They're already selling — the question is how well.

### Downtown Committee of Syracuse
`downtownsyracuse.com`

Administers the **Syracuse Cultural Festivals Fund** on behalf of the city. They don't just run events — they *fund* them, which means they know every festival organiser in Syracuse. Another list-multiplier.

## The timing

**Organisations sell next year's sponsorship in the autumn.** September through November is the season. It starts in four weeks, which means your outreach should land in early September — right as they're building next year's plan and before budgets are set.

That's the best timing signal in any version of this business we've looked at.

## Questions for the call

1. How much sponsorship revenue did you raise last year, and from how many partners?
2. What do you currently send a prospective sponsor?
3. Who does the asking — staff, board, or volunteers?
4. When do you start selling next year's sponsorship?
5. Is there budget for materials, or would this come out of a programme line?

Question 3 matters most. **If a board member does sponsorship sales as a volunteer, they will love you** — you're removing the part of the job they dread. If a paid development director does it, they may see you as a threat. Ask early.

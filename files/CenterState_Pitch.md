## 3. The email

> **Subject:** Made you a vertical from the Micron talk
>
> Hi Katie,
>
> I'm Ima — I'm a Television, Radio and Film student over at Newhouse.
>
> I was watching Manish Bhatia's talk from your annual meeting and got a little carried away: I cut a 45-second vertical out of it, captions burned in, sized for LinkedIn and Reels. It's here: [link]. Yours to post if it's useful — no strings at all.
>
> I picked that one because Micron is what everybody around here is talking about, and the best bit is buried twenty minutes into a twenty-eight-minute video where hardly anyone's going to find it. There are easily eight more moments that good in there, and a few in every Talk CNY episode.
>
> I did notice you've posted a couple of Shorts before, so I know this isn't a new idea to you — my guess is it's more a time thing than an interest thing. If it'd help to have someone making them regularly, I'd love to talk. And since we're both in Syracuse, I'm glad to come to you — coffee's on me.
>
> Either way, take the clip. Thanks for making the show.
>
> Ima Kennerly
> Newhouse School, Syracuse University
> [phone] · [email]

### Why it's built this way

**"Got a little carried away"** does the most work in the whole email. It reframes unpaid spec work as enthusiasm rather than a sales tactic, which is both warmer and closer to true.

**The Shorts line is the one that matters strategically.** She has made two. If you write as though the gap is total, she'll correct you and you've lost credibility in the first reply. Naming it — and putting it down to *time* rather than *interest* — is accurate, respectful, and brings her real objection ("we tried, it didn't take off") into the open instead of leaving it to fester.

**"Coffee's on me"** is your one structural advantage over every freelancer who'll email her this year. Nobody else can offer to walk over.

**The clip goes first, unconditionally.** Proof beats a promise when you have no track record.

### Two honesty checks

Only keep **"thanks for making the show"** if you've actually listened to it. She hosts it — she'll be able to tell in thirty seconds of conversation, and a hollow compliment is worse than none.

Same with the enthusiasm generally: warmth only works if it's real. If Bhatia's talk didn't interest you, cut a different video. The email is easy to write when the clip is one you wanted to make.
